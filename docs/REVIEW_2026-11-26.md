# Project Review — Mini Newsroom (Port Wexley)

**Date:** 2026-11-26  
**Scope:** Read-only architectural, creative, and operational review of `newsroom/` (~25k LOC), `entertainment/` (~11.5k LOC), `tools/` (~2.7k LOC), `tests/` (~16k LOC across 119 test files), and `docs/`.

---

## Executive Summary

**Mini Newsroom (Port Wexley)** is an unusually mature piece of generative systems engineering. Most "always-on AI stream" projects stop at a novelty loop of stateless prompts and robotic voices; this feels like an actual regional British television broadcaster because every layer—real-time scheduling, editorial memory, deterministic town simulation, CPU voice synthesis, pixel-art composition, and operational safety—has been hardened against real 12-hour overnight runs.

What sets it apart most is the development loop documented in [HANDOVER.md](HANDOVER.md): running full nights into OBS, auditing logs and contact sheets the next morning, replaying failed slots against pre-night backups, and shipping targeted structural fixes before the next broadcast.

---

## What Stands Out (Strengths)

### 1. Real-Time Playout Discipline ("The broadcast never waits on the AI")
- **Decoupled pipeline:** The boundary across [`newsroom/producer.py`](../newsroom/producer.py), [`newsroom/buffer.py`](../newsroom/buffer.py), [`newsroom/workers.py`](../newsroom/workers.py), and [`newsroom/player.py`](../newsroom/player.py) is clean and battle-tested:
  - The ready side of `Buffer` is indexed by `slot_id` rather than a FIFO queue, so one slow model call never blocks finished segments behind it.
  - `Producer` computes its planning cursor from *actual* remaining on-air time plus pending buffer depth (using real WAV durations once voiced, targets before), preventing target-duration drift from snowballing over hours.
  - Multi-call commissions (specials and the 15-minute nightly documentary) run as low-priority, checkpointed state machines (`*.partial.json`) that re-queue after each stage so live slots always interleave ahead of them.
- **Graceful degradation:** If live generation falls behind or an API key fails, the station steps smoothly through continuity cards/idents $\rightarrow$ pre-voiced local adverts $\rightarrow$ music-bed filler $\rightarrow$ weather replay, backed by deterministic template stand-ins per show and per-subsystem frame fault isolation (`Guard` in [`newsroom/__main__.py`](../newsroom/__main__.py)).

### 2. Neuro-Symbolic Split ("Code owns arithmetic; LLMs own voice")
- Offloading everything language models hallucinate—tide tables and foot-ferry crossings ([`newsroom/tides.py`](../newsroom/tides.py)), double round-robin fixtures and seeded Poisson match scores ([`newsroom/sport.py`](../newsroom/sport.py), `rugby.py`, `darts.py`), fish auction landings (`quay.py`), council votes and dated promises (`chamber.py`, `promises.py`), pub-quiz/darts scoring (`entertainment/quiz.py`, `oche.py`), and *High Water*'s push-your-luck mechanics (`entertainment/highwater.py`, balanced via thousands of simulated games)—eliminates numeric and continuity drift while leaving the writers free to focus on character and small-town texture.

### 3. Multi-Tiered Editorial Governance & Persistent Memory
- **Layered back office:** Stateless live writers stay coherent because background editors curate the shared board during idle worker ticks:
  - **Story Desk** ([`newsroom/desk.py`](../newsroom/desk.py)) advances changed threads in bounded batches, manages phases (`emerging` $\rightarrow$ `closed` / `faded`), and parks future-dated developments in the **running-stories waiting pool**.
  - **Senior Editor** ([`newsroom/senior.py`](../newsroom/senior.py)) reviews the whole board every two hours (only once the town has actually moved) to curb convergence, merge duplicates, and direct pooled threads.
  - **Creative Editor** ([`entertainment/creative.py`](../entertainment/creative.py)) vets plans before air and critiques aired Channel 8 programmes afterward.
  - **Editors' Meeting** ([`newsroom/meeting.py`](../newsroom/meeting.py)) brings both channels' editors together once a day to avoid double-booking residents, pass real events across channels, and settle shared canon.
- **Canon firewalling:** Treating *Marina Row* and the banked TV movies as in-universe fiction (`FICTION_SHOWS`) whose actors live in Port Wexley—and exposing aired soap cliffhangers to Channel 7 solely as viewer small talk via `talk.py`—is a great worldbuilding design.

### 4. Pragmatic Cost, Audio, and Visual Engineering
- **~$1–2/day operating cost:** Task-based model routing in [`config.toml`](../config.toml) (Haiku live, DeepSeek Flash thinking in the back office with Sonnet fallback, Opus reserved for a handful of nightly editorial calls) plus explicit `CACHE_BREAK` and incremental `CACHE_BLOCK` prompt prefix caching in [`newsroom/llm.py`](../newsroom/llm.py) keeps 24/7 cloud inference affordable.
- **CPU-first voice pipeline:** Running Kyutai's Pocket TTS (cloning DramaBox and Kokoro reference clips) with automatic per-line Kokoro fallback on CPU ([`newsroom/tts.py`](../newsroom/tts.py)) leaves the GPU free, while RMS loudness matching, FFT telephone-band filtering for callers, clause-breathing token splits, and ear-tuned Cornish/heteronym respellings (`names.toml`, `_TWO_WAY`) solve real broadcast-audio rough edges.
- **Hybrid pixel rendering:** Drawing scenes and deterministic `Look`-based character sprites on a `320x180` nearest-neighbour canvas ([`newsroom/scenes.py`](../newsroom/scenes.py), [`newsroom/sprites.py`](../newsroom/sprites.py)) while rendering crisp native-resolution typography in `present()` gives authentic pixel art without sacrificing caption legibility on YouTube/Twitch encodes.
- **Operational & legal polish:** `safety.py` checks both scripted text (including unvoiced teletext/titles) and `tts.synthesize`, `control.py` provides an instant F12/file test-card kill switch, [`tools/story_browser.py`](../tools/story_browser.py) inspects live state without importing `newsroom` or holding file locks on Windows, and [`LICENSE-CONTENT.md`](../LICENSE-CONTENT.md) cleanly separates the MIT engine from Port Wexley's IP.

## Architectural Trade-Offs, Debt & Risks Worth Watching

### 1. Concurrent Multi-Process World State (`data/world.json`)
- [LAUNCH.md](LAUNCH.md) notes the option of airing Channel 8 via a second station process (`--channel entertainment`) alongside Channel 7.
- Although `world.save()` safely retries atomic `.tmp` $\rightarrow$ `replace` swaps on Windows file locks, each process loads `World` into memory once at startup and periodically writes its entire in-memory snapshot back to disk. **Two station processes pointed at the same `data/world.json` simultaneously would overwrite each other's threads, archive, people, and bookings.**
- Alternating nights (or running Channel 8 in scheduled windows while Channel 7 is stopped) is completely safe; running both channels *simultaneously* on the same town will require either a single multi-channel coordinator process, split per-channel state files, or a lightweight local state server/SQLite store.

### 2. Global Mutable Registries & Import-Time `CONFIG`
- `CONFIG` reads environment variables at module import time, which requires careful late imports in [`newsroom/__main__.py`](../newsroom/__main__.py) and defensive `sys.modules` checks in [`tests/conftest.py`](../tests/conftest.py).
- [`entertainment/__init__.py`](../entertainment/__init__.py) plugs Channel 8 into `newsroom/` by mutating ~20 module-level dicts and sets (`SHOW_SYSTEMS`, `PROMPT_HOOKS`, `CHECK_HOOKS`, `VIEW_HOOKS`, `SEATS`, `PINNED_VOICES`, `TICKER_AFTER`, etc.) and returning manual `undo()` closures for test isolation.
- That kept velocity high for a second package in one repo, and the 119-file test suite guards it well, but each new show now takes ~35 lines of scattered hook registration where forgetting one `pop`/`discard` in `undo()` leaks global state. Consolidating those ~20 hooks into a single declarative `ShowSpec` / plugin object will simplify both registration and the eventual engine split described in [ENGINE.md](ENGINE.md).

### 3. Gravitational Growth in `writers.py` (~2,960 LOC) and `world.py` (~1,780 LOC)
- Both modules carry heavy multi-concern load: `World` now holds ~40 top-level fields (including show-specific booking lists like `kettles`, `slipways`, `highwaters`, `quizzes`), and `writers.py` combines prompt templates, guest casting, schema dispatch, and post-generation text repairs.
- Post-generation validators (`tells_next_as_done`, `trails_gone_guest`, `link_echoes`, `rested_line`, `CHECK_HOOKS`) are individually backed by real night-log evidence, which is great. Two specific rough edges to watch as these accumulate:
  - **Retry vs. immediate template fallback in `CHECK_HOOKS`:** Some show checks raise `LLMError` instead of `AskAgain`, throwing away a near-miss script for a short canned template without giving the model a single corrective retry (e.g., *The Coast Road* speaker check noted in [HANDOVER.md](HANDOVER.md)). Defaulting show-level semantic checks to one `AskAgain` retry would preserve far more model-written segments.
  - **Pocket TTS runaway / length-cap lines:** Night logs note occasional lines hitting Pocket's max generation cap and either cutting off or trailing into babble (e.g., 21 words taking 17.4s). Adding a sanity guard in `tts.synthesize()` on rendered `duration_s` vs. word/character count—falling back to Kokoro or re-splitting when Pocket stalls or babbles—would catch audio anomalies automatically before air.

### 4. Pre-Launch Housekeeping Before 24 December
- **Documentation lag behind Channel 8:** [README.md](../README.md), [LAUNCH.md](LAUNCH.md), and [`docs/launch/making-of.md`](launch/making-of.md) understate how much of Channel 8 is already built and airing (*High Water*, *The Slipway*, six banked 80-minute TV movies, and *The Late News* handover via `Show.then`).
- **Known test failures on HEAD:** [HANDOVER.md](HANDOVER.md) notes two failing tests in `tests/test_highwater_show.py` (`test_nobody_climbs_again_soon_or_after_another_channel_8_show` and `..._a_short_list_is_made_up`) worth resolving before the public soak night.
- **Channel 8 days in the Annals:** As noted on 2026-11-26 in [HANDOVER.md](HANDOVER.md), a pure Channel 8 day currently advances no news threads, leaving that day's annals thin for Sunday's *The Week in Port Wexley*. Letting the story desk or an off-air morning summary tick lightly—or having *The Late News* / annals acknowledge quiet news days—will keep Sunday reviews balanced when alternating channels.

---

## Overall Verdict

Port Wexley is one of the strongest examples to date of turning LLMs from a chat interface into an **autonomous, stateful, low-cost creative medium**. The architecture respects hard real-time constraints, treats model weaknesses honestly with code, and backs creative ambition with disciplined engineering and testing. With the small pre-launch items above tidied up, it is in great shape for its Christmas Eve debut.
